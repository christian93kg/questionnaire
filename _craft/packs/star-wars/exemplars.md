# Exemplars — star-wars

Doctrine: `quiz-question.card.md` → `## Exemplars`. Four below are diegetic; #5 is
world-tinted (register only), so the spectrum stays visible.

---

**1 — the mismatched manifest.** Pressure: an occupying authority, paperwork as a weapon.
No capitalised words to delete — nothing here needs a name to answer.

> The garrison at the checkpoint wants your manifest, and it doesn't match the cargo.
> - Hand over the real manifest and take what's coming.
> - Pay the difference in credits, quietly.
> - Talk fast; the discrepancy was theirs, not yours.
> - Turn around before you reach the gate.

**2 — the wrong answers.** Pressure: machines are people-ish. The cost sits with the
droid, not just the taker — an "old laptop" version can't generate that.

> The droid that's flown with you for six years starts giving wrong answers, and the fix
> means wiping half its memory.
> - Wipe it and hope the fix holds.
> - Patch around the fault and let it get worse slowly.
> - Retire it. Fly with a stranger unit instead.
> - Keep flying with the wrong answers. You've learned to check them.

**3 — three days out.** Pressure: distance is fatal; nobody is coming to help. Built
entirely from register — part, comm — with nothing named.

> The part you need is three days out, and nobody's answering the comm.
> - Fly it broken. You'll feel every wrong part of that decision.
> - Wait it out and burn the days you don't have.
> - Strip a working part from something else you need.
> - Signal for a tow, and owe whoever answers.

**4 — the feeling she can't explain.** Pressure: an intuition some trust, others sneer
at — never named as a power the taker has, which keeps it diegetic, not a role grant.

> Your co-pilot wants to hold the launch from the hangar on a feeling she can't explain,
> and the docking slot won't come around again today.
> - Hold it. She's been right before.
> - Take off anyway. Feelings aren't fuel.
> - Ask her to name one specific thing, and hold only for that.
> - Split it: you go, she stays behind to be sure.

**5 — the collector on the comm.** World-tinted, deliberately: debt to criminals is
ordinary here, but the dilemma is an ordinary loan-shark situation. Delete "Hutt's" — the
one capitalised word — and it survives untouched: atmosphere, not structure.

> You owe the Hutt's collector for the fuel that got you here, and he's on the comm
> asking for the favor back today.
> - Pay it off, and take longer routes for a while.
> - Do the favor. Once, and call it done.
> - Stall for two days and see who blinks first.
> - Skip town before he tries again.
