# Exemplars — quiz questions

> **These five units are the highest-fidelity statement of the target voice.** They are
> not illustrations of the card; they are the thing the card is trying to describe, and
> they outrank any prose description of the register — including the card's own tests.
> When the card and an exemplar disagree, the exemplar is right and the card needs work.
>
> **Cap: five entries.** Sworn: when a new unit beats one of these, it is **swapped in
> for the weakest, never appended**. A growing exemplars file is a style guide, and a
> style guide is read once; five units are re-read every session. The cap is the feature.
>
> These are also the known-good fixture in prose form: `.claude/hooks/question-lint.py`
> must run clean on this file. A rule that fires here is wrong; the unit is not.

---

**1 — the institutional ask.** Register: the six years is the one telling particular; every
option is an enacted act and none of them is free.

> The organisation you gave six years to asks you for something you think is wrong.
> - Refuse, in writing, with your name on it.
> - Do it, and start building the case that stops it recurring.
> - Do it. You don't get to pick which parts you believe in.
> - Leave. Not loudly. Just stop being available.

**2 — the sixteenth hour.** Register: the prompt asks the taker to report a fact about
themselves without ever naming the trait; four genuinely different reasons, same act.

> Sixteen hours into the emergency. What's actually keeping you there?
> - Two specific people who'd be in trouble if I left.
> - I said I'd stay. That's the whole reason.
> - The problem itself. It's interesting and it's mine now.
> - If I go, it lands on someone who can't carry it.

**3 — the credit.** Register: hands back on a want-at-risk — the meeting is still running,
the outcome undecided. Nobody's option is costless.

> Someone senior repeats your idea in the meeting and the room credits them.
> - Say it now, while the room is still on it.
> - Let it land. Bring the receipts to them after.
> - Nothing. The idea shipped. That was the point.
> - Nothing in the room. Everyone who matters already knows.

**4 — the shortcut.** Register: an unglamorous, procedural situation; the wrong answer is
not signposted, and two of the four are defensible in a review.

> The fix works. You can't explain why, and shipping day is tomorrow.
> - Ship it. Write down exactly what you don't know.
> - Hold it. Unexplained isn't fixed.
> - Ship it and keep digging on your own time.
> - Hand it to whoever can explain it, and say so.

**5 — the quiet withdrawal.** Register: displacement — the subject is trust, and the word
never appears; it sits in the calendar and the phone instead.

> Someone you rely on has been slower to answer for three weeks. Nothing's been said.
> - Ask them straight out what changed.
> - Nothing. They'll say it when they're ready.
> - Stop relying on them. Quietly reroute the work.
> - Book the thing you always do. See who shows.
